# FlavorDB2 and Kew evaluation for under-covered spices

Research checked 2026-10-17 against the primary resources linked below. This review uses the existing FlavorDB2 entity JSON cache and coverage file, then checks the live FlavorDB2 search/entity pages and Kew's Plants of the World Online (POWO) / World Checklist of Vascular Plants (WCVP) pages. “Not verified” means no defensible record was identified in the material checked; it does not prove that a record is absent from FlavorDB2.

## What the sources provide

[FlavorDB2](https://cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/flavordb2/) associates ingredient entities with reported flavor molecules. Its entity pages include PubChem IDs, flavor-profile words, ingredient synonyms, category and a natural-source field; linked molecule records contain molecular identity and physicochemical, taste, odor and functional-group descriptors. The [entity-search guide](https://cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/flavordb2/how_to_use) describes name/synonym search and entity details. Ingredient pages expose JSON through their Download action / `entities_json?id=…` endpoint. The site states the dataset is [CC BY-NC-SA 3.0](https://cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/flavordb2/faq): attribution and ShareAlike apply, and commercial use is not allowed without separate permission. Counts below refer to distinct molecule PubChem IDs in the repository's existing cache where available; they are not concentration measurements, and an entity's molecule inventory does not establish the aroma contribution or amount in a prepared food.

Kew's [POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/) supplies accepted plant names, synonyms, hierarchy, and distribution backed by WCVP; POWO says its name and distribution backbone is refreshed weekly ([About POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/about)). It is valuable for matching common spice names to accepted botanical taxa and avoiding false equivalences. It does not identify culinary form, cultivar identity, plant part used, drying/smoking/fermentation, or culinary origin. For downloadable taxonomy, Kew points to the latest WCVP data through its [data page/repository](https://powo.science.kew.org/cite-us). The GBIF WCVP listing currently states [CC BY 4.0](https://www.gbif.org/dataset/f382f0ce-323a-4091-bb9f-add557f3a9a2), while current POWO taxon-page source attributions label WCVP as CC BY 3.0; Kew's [terms](https://www.kew.org/science/collections-and-resources/data-and-digital/terms-of-use) say dataset-specific licence fields govern and third-party contributed data retain their owner's terms. Treat these as version/record-specific statements, inspect the licence and README bundled with the particular WCVP download, and preserve its attribution. Do not assume every descriptive record, image, or contributed POWO page is covered by the WCVP dataset licence.

The count reflects deduplication by PubChem ID, consistent with `ingest_flavordb2.py`; FlavorDB2 rows sometimes repeat or contain several flavor terms per molecule. Descriptors are attached to compounds, not ingredient-level aroma measurements. The existing pipeline's 30-molecule cutoff is a project heuristic, not a FlavorDB2 quality guarantee; identity/form fit should come first.

## Recommendation

FlavorDB2 is useful for adding molecule identity and flavor-word/compound descriptors when a sufficiently populated entity matches the culinary ingredient. Recommend five exact entities for integration: Asafoetida 376 (37), Carom Seed/Ajwain 381 (155), Curry Leaf 385 (31), Summer savory 444 (122), and Epazote 708 (93). Keep Spearmint 266 (237) marked approximate because its JSON alias explicitly says spearmint oil. The remaining seven candidates stay unmapped. In particular, do not borrow the current green cardamom or generic Capsicum entries.

Kew/WCVP is a strong low-risk source for accepted botanical names, synonym resolution, and broad native/introduced distribution. It can improve ingredient-to-taxon links and prevent false mappings, but it cannot supply compound overlap or recipe-origin claims. Keep the taxonomy record and culinary ingredient/form as separate facts, and do not infer cuisine origin from a plant's native range.
